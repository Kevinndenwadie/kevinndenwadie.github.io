## Job Market Paper
<ins>Informality and Monetary Policy Revisited: The Role of Endogenous Separations</ins>
>**Abstract:** This paper identifies a novel channel through which informality alters monetary policy transmission in developing countries. By internalizing the job separation decision within the formal sector, I demonstrate that the presence of an informal economy amplifies the probability of firm-worker match severance by a factor of 1.5 following a contractionary monetary surprise. This mechanism remains unexplored in the existing literature, which predominantly relies on exogenous separation rates, thereby overlooking the strategic severance decisions between firms and workers. Motivated by empirical evidence showing that job destruction is highly countercyclical, I develop a New Keynesian DSGE model featuring a dual labor market calibrated to Mexico. The formal sector is characterized by search-and-matching frictions, staggered nominal wage contracts and price setting, and endogenous job destruction based on idiosyncratic match productivity, while the informal sector is modeled as fully competitive and flexible. I show that the informal sector acts as a shadow outside option that interacts with formal nominal rigidities to drive up the reservation formal job severance. This endogenous acceleration of labor flows out of the formal sector significantly hinders the monetary authority’s effectiveness, resulting in a substantially higher
sacrifice ratio compared to a formal-only economy.
